The North Riding Quarter Sessions was the court through which Justices of the Peace administered both criminal justice and local government across this part of Yorkshire, meeting four times a year (Epiphany, Easter, Midsummer, Michaelmas) from at least 1605 until the newly-created North Riding County Council took over its administrative role in 1889. Its business ranged from criminal trials to roads, licensing, the poor law, and the militia. Summary Convictions — the records this site draws from — cover the minor end of that criminal work: offences dealt with directly by Justices in Petty Sessions, each recorded with the offender, the offence, and the punishment given. See the [NYCRO blog](https://nycroblog.com/2024/11/29/records-of-the-north-riding-quarter-sessions/) for more on the court's history, and its [follow-up post](https://nycroblog.com/2025/05/28/quarter-sessions-bundles/) on the Quarter Sessions Bundles specifically.
